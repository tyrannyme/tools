On the northern cape, where the cliffs fall into the grey water, stands a tower older than the town below. Every evening, as the sky turns to rose, the keeper climbs the stairs with oil and matches, counting all one hundred and twelve steps. At the top he trims the wick and polishes the lens. When the first star appears, the lamp is lit and its beam sweeps the bay in long, slow arcs. He keeps the flame steady through the night, and by morning every ship passes safely beneath the light. Then he climbs down, writes the weather in his log, sleeps until noon, and begins again, as his father did, and his father before him.
